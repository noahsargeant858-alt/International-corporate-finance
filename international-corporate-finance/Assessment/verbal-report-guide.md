# Verbal Report — Preparation Guide
**Week commencing 23–27 November 2026 | 15 minutes | 20%**

---

## The Format (Burned Into Your Brain)

```
Minute 0:00  →  You enter Teams waiting room (15 mins early)
Minute 0:00  →  Dr Osiolo asks: "So, what do you recommend, bring me up to date?"
Minutes 0–5  →  YOUR 5 minutes. Non-negotiable. She will stop you at 5:00.
Minutes 5–15 →  Her 10 minutes of questions. You defend.
```

---

## Your 5 Minutes — What to Cover

You have roughly **1 minute per bullet point:**

1. **Clear recommendation** — Option 1 or Option 2 (say it in the first 30 seconds. Don't build up to it.)
2. **Comparison of 1 vs 2** — key numbers side by side (NPV, IRR, risk profile)
3. **Method of finance** — which financing route and why (one sentence, backed by WACC)
4. **Option 1 specifics** — which platform supplier (British or Munchen) and which material supplier (and why)
5. **Option 2 specifics** — Merge or Acquire, at what price, financed how

> **Do not waste a single second on definitions, theory, or scene-setting.** She already knows what NPV is. Get straight to your recommendation and justify it with numbers.

---

## The 10-Minute Q&A — What She'll Likely Ask

Prepare answers to every one of these:

### On Option 1
- "Why that discount rate / WACC figure?"
- "What assumptions did you make about the oil price?"
- "Why British/German supplier rather than the other?"
- "Why Russia/USA/Netherlands for materials?"
- "What happens to your NPV if oil prices drop 20%?"
- "How did you handle the sunk costs?"
- "Why that particular financing method?"
- "What are the main risks and how do you mitigate them?"
- "What does the critical path tell you?"

### On Option 2
- "How did you value Arabic Oil?"
- "Why merge rather than acquire (or vice versa)?"
- "How does the 30% remittance restriction affect your analysis?"
- "What's the goodwill figure and is it justified?"
- "How does a 0% Abu Dhabi tax rate change the economics?"
- "What are the integration risks?"
- "Did you consider transfer pricing opportunities?"

### On Finance
- "Why not issue a bond instead of a loan?"
- "What's your calculated WACC under each financing option?"
- "Why is the rights issue priced at £1.00–£1.25?"
- "What is the value of the right in a rights issue?"

### Curveball (she might do this)
- "I disagree with your recommendation — I think Option 2 is better. Change my mind."
- "What if the oil price falls to $50 a barrel tomorrow?"
- This is not a trick — stay calm, justify your evidence, or say "that's a fair point, and for my written report I'll explore that scenario further."

## The PowerPoint Slides

**You need slides BUT this is NOT a presentation.** The slides are a visual aid, not a script.

**Suggested structure (5–6 slides maximum):**

| Slide | Content |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Recommendation** — Option X | Finance by Y | Bold statement up top |
| 2 | **Option 1 vs Option 2 comparison table** — NPV, IRR, ARR, Payback side by side |
| 3 | **Option 1 key numbers** — cash flow summary, NPV, recommended suppliers |
| 4 | **Option 2 key numbers** — valuation, merger vs acquisition comparison, remittance impact |
| 5 | **Finance recommendation** — WACC comparison, recommended method |
| 6 | **Key risks** — one column per option, top 3 risks each |

**Design rules:**
- Numbers > words
- Font big enough to see on Teams without squinting
- Bring the data, not the story — she can read

## What "Defending" Looks Like

She says: *"I think the German platform is better value even with the FX risk."*

**Bad answer:** "Oh yeah, maybe you're right." ← immediate marks loss

**Good answer:** "At today's EUR/GBP rate of [X], €355M converts to approximately £[Y]M — that's £[Z]M more than the British quote. To justify that premium you'd need the euro to depreciate by [%] over the construction period, which I don't think is realistic given [current ECB/BoE rate outlook]. The FX risk also adds uncertainty to the advance payment. That's why I recommend British Oil Machinery."

> **The key:** back every statement with a number or a credible reference. Opinions without evidence get challenged into the ground. Numbers with sources can be defended.
