## 3. Financial Management Framework

> **Definition from the slides:**
> "The management of all the processes associated with the efficient acquisition and deployment of both short-term and long-term financial resources involving financial planning and financial control with the objective of **maximising shareholders' wealth**."

Two fundamental decisions that underpin everything in this module:

| Decision | Question | Tools Used |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| **Investment Decision** | Where should we invest? | NPV, IRR, ARR, Payback |
| **Financing Decision** | How should we fund it? | WACC, CAPM, Rights Issue, Bonds, Loans |

> **Key principle from the slides:** *"Finance is not free. Debt has an interest cost; equity has a required return. Therefore, financing choice affects the required return from the investment."*

This means: you can't evaluate Option 1's NPV without first calculating the correct WACC — and the WACC changes depending on how you finance it. The investment and financing decisions are interlinked.

## 4. The NPV Decision Framework

From slide 18 — the logical chain for your investment appraisal:

```
Step 1: Forecast the cash flow over the project life
Step 2: Calculate WACC (minimum hurdle rate)
Step 3: Calculate NPV using WACC as discount rate
Step 4: Calculate IRR (the rate at which NPV = £0)

Decision rules:
  +NPV and IRR > WACC → Invest ✓
  -NPV and IRR < WACC → Don't invest ✗
```

**But NPV is only as good as its inputs — always stress test:**
- What oil price did you assume? What if it drops?
- What production volume?
- What CAPEX figure?
- What operating costs?
- What exchange rate?
- What discount rate (WACC)?

Slide 19 example: *"You invest £1M, NPV @ WACC of 13.4% is £10,000. Is it worth it? YES — a +NPV adds value, even a small one."* But — how robust is that £10,000? If the oil price drops 5%, does it flip negative? That sensitivity analysis is what earns marks.

## 5. THE MOST IMPORTANT SECTION: How to Write the Report

Dr Osiolo is extremely clear about what she wants and what gets **0%**. Burn this into your brain.

### The "M6 Motorway" Rule

**Bad answer (0%):**

*"There are advantages and disadvantages to both Fixed and Floating rate loans."*
Then a table with Advantages on the left, Disadvantages on the right.

This is **Mark = 0%** — not because it's wrong, but because it doesn't evaluate anything.

**Good answer:**

*"Recommendation: Fixed rate loan at 7% p.a."*

*"Justification: At the current Bank of England base rate of X%, a variable rate loan at Base + 2% currently costs X%. However, with the BoE expected to [raise/maintain/cut] rates over the project horizon (BoE MPC, 2026), fixing at 7% provides cost certainty for the critical early years of the project when cash flow is most vulnerable. The £1M fixing fee is recovered within [X months] of rate savings under the projected rate path."*

The formula:
```
Evaluate → Recommend → Justify → Support with Evidence
```

### What She Does NOT Want
- Definitions of technical terms (she knows what NPV is)
- Explanations of how you calculated things
- Tables of advantages and disadvantages without a recommendation
- Waffle introductions
- Padding that doesn't help the Board make a decision

### What She DOES Want
- Recommendation first (state it clearly in the opening of each section)
- Justification with specific numbers and real-time data
- Evidence from credible sources (Bank of England, ONS, EIA, FT)
- Full evaluation of ALL options (not just the one you recommend)
