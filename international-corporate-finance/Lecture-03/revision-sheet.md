## Rights Issue — Full Worked Method

**Setup:** 1-for-5 Rights Issue at £2/share. Market price £2.50. 500,000 shares in issue.

```
New shares = 500,000 ÷ 5 = 100,000
Value of the Right = Market Price − Issue Price = £2.50 − £2.00 = £0.50
Cash raised = 100,000 × £2 = £200,000
Share Premium = (Issue Price − Nominal Value) × new shares = £1 × 100,000 = £100,000
```

**TERP formula:** `TERP = (N × cum-rights price + issue price) ÷ (N+1)`, N = shares needed per new share.

**Dilution check:** if you own X% before and don't take up your rights, your % falls because the share count rises while your own holding stays fixed. If you DO exercise, your % is unchanged.

**⚠️ Retained Profit is NOT cash** — it's what the company owes shareholders, not money it can spend. Never suggest "just use retained profits" as if it were sitting in the bank.

## Debt — Quick Reference

| Term | One-liner |
|---|---|
| **Eurobond** | A bond in a currency foreign to where it's issued — NOT a euro-bond |
| **Coupon** | The annual interest rate on a bond |
| **Par / Above / Below Par** | Face value / sold for more (attractive coupon) / sold for less (unattractive coupon) |
| **Zero Coupon Bond** | 0% interest, bought at a discount, full value paid at redemption |
| **Credit Rating** | AAA = safest (0% default). Below BB = "junk" |

**Why borrow in $ instead of £:** need to pay a $ cost (natural hedge), eliminate FX risk on that cost, or the $ interest rate is simply lower. **But** borrowing $ means repaying $ — you've swapped one FX exposure for another unless it offsets an existing $ cost.

## SPV/SPE — Quick Reference

| Term | One-liner |
|---|---|
| **SPV/SPE** | Separate legal entity financing one project, shielding the parent from liability |
| **Recourse** | Lenders CAN pursue shareholders' other assets on default |
| **Non-Recourse** | Lenders CANNOT — claim limited to the SPE itself (the usual structure) |
| **Off-balance-sheet** | SPE debt doesn't appear on the parent's own balance sheet |

**Construction-phase risk:** no revenue exists until the project is operational — all parties carry risk before debt service can even begin.

## Flash Card Q&A

**Q: Why can't a company just use retained profits instead of a rights issue?**
A: Retained profit isn't cash — it's a record of profit already owed to shareholders, typically already spent on assets.

**Q: What's the difference between the Primary and Secondary market?**
A: Primary = company receives cash (new shares issued). Secondary = investors trade existing shares between themselves; the company gets nothing from this.

**Q: A bond pays a coupon below the current market rate. Will it trade above or below par?**
A: Below par — investors will only buy an unattractive coupon at a discount to compensate.

**Q: What's the single biggest marking risk in the financing section of your report?**
A: Listing advantages/disadvantages of financing options instead of giving a justified recommendation based on evaluating them — explicitly a zero-mark answer.

**Q: Why might new debt for the North Sea platform cost more than UK Oil's existing 6–7% debt?**
A: Because it adds to an already-geared balance sheet (Equity £1,925M vs Debt £700M) — higher gearing raises the perceived risk for all lenders, pushing up the rate on new borrowing specifically.
