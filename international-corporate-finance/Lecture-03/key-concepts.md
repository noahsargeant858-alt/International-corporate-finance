## Equity

### IPO (Initial Public Offering)
**What it is:** The process of a private company offering shares to the public for the first time, via the **Primary Market**, raising new capital directly for the company.

### Primary Market vs Secondary Market
**Primary Market:** Where a company sells **new** shares and receives the cash directly (an IPO, a Rights Issue).
**Secondary Market:** Where existing shares trade between investors afterwards — the company receives **no cash** from these trades, regardless of the price.

### Rights Issue
**What it is:** An already-listed company offers existing shareholders the right to buy new shares, in proportion to their current holding, usually at a discount to the market price. This is how a listed company (not an IPO candidate) raises new equity.

**Value of the Right = Current Market Price − Issue Price**

### Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP)
**Formula:** `TERP = (N × cum-rights price + issue price) ÷ (N + 1)`
**What it is:** The theoretical new share price immediately after a rights issue, once the discounted new shares are factored in. N = number of existing shares needed to buy one new share.

### Dilution
**What it is:** The reduction in a shareholder's percentage ownership that occurs if they **don't** take up their rights in a rights issue — the company issues more shares, but their own holding stays the same size, so it becomes a smaller slice of a bigger pie.

### Retained Profit (the trap)
**What it is NOT:** Cash available to spend.
**What it actually is:** A record of profit already earned and *not yet* paid out to shareholders — representing what the company already owes them, not new money it can raise.

## Debt

### Eurobond
**What it is:** A bond issued in a currency **foreign** to the country where it's issued — NOT a bond denominated in euros. E.g. a dollar bond issued in London is a Eurobond.

### Coupon Rate
**What it is:** The interest rate paid annually on a bond, based on its face (par) value.

### Par Value / Above Par / Below Par
**Par:** The bond's face value (what it's redeemed for).
**Above Par:** Sold for more than face value — the coupon is attractive relative to current rates.
**Below Par:** Sold for less than face value — the coupon is unattractive relative to current rates.

### Zero Coupon Bond
**What it is:** A bond paying 0% interest, sold at a discount to its face value — the investor's entire return comes from the gap between the discounted purchase price and the full redemption value.

### Credit Rating
**What it is:** An assessment (by Moody's, S&P or Fitch) of how likely a bond issuer is to default. AAA = safest (0% historical default rate). Below BB = "junk" — high yield compensates for high risk.

## Project Finance

### SPV / SPE (Special Purpose Vehicle / Entity)
**What it is:** A separate legal entity created specifically to finance one project, shielding the parent company(ies) from the project's liabilities.

### Recourse Finance
**What it is:** Lenders can pursue the shareholders' own wider assets if the SPE defaults.

### Non-Recourse (or Limited Recourse) Finance
**What it is:** Lenders' claim is limited to the SPE itself — the shareholders' other assets are protected. This is the typical SPE structure.

### Off-Balance-Sheet Finance
**What it is:** Debt held inside a separate legal entity (like an SPE) that does not have to appear on the parent company's own balance sheet.

## Gearing

### Gearing / Leverage
**What it is:** The proportion of debt relative to equity in a company's capital structure. Higher gearing = higher financial risk = typically higher interest rates demanded on *new* borrowing.

### The core gearing principle
**The rule:** The more a company borrows, the higher its gearing, the higher lenders perceive the risk of non-repayment, and the higher the interest rate they will charge on any further new debt.

## Assessment Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Share Premium** | The amount paid for a new share above its nominal (face) value |
| **Book Value / Net Worth** | Total assets minus total liabilities — what the company is "worth" on paper |
| **Capital Allowance** | A UK tax deduction for capital investment, given instead of depreciation (Capital Allowances Act 2001) |
| **Covenant** | A condition attached to a loan restricting what the borrower can do, or requiring certain financial ratios to be maintained |
| **Lien** | A lender's legal right to seize a specific asset if a borrower defaults |
